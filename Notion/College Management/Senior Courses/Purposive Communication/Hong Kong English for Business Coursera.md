# 📖 Lecture: Understanding Business Writing Genres

### Recap: What is a Genre?

Before we go into **business writing genres**, let us revisit the foundation.

In **linguistics**, the term *genre* refers to:

> **“a socially agreed upon and recognized form of communication that has been developed over time to communicate more effectively and efficiently.”**

🔎 **Side Note (Key Insight):**

* *Socially agreed upon* means that genres are not arbitrary. They evolve within a community or profession, and everyone in that group recognizes them.
* *Developed over time* means they are shaped by repeated use until conventions (rules and expectations) are formed.
* *More effective and efficient* means genres reduce misunderstandings: people know what to expect and how to interpret messages.

For example, in **speech genres**:

* A **telephone conversation** has recognizable conventions—greetings (“Hello, this is…”), development (“The reason I’m calling is…”), and closing (“Thank you, goodbye”).
* If you skip these conventions, the interaction feels abrupt or even impolite.

In **written genres**, the same applies:

* **Medical charts** for doctors, **legal briefs** for lawyers, and **business letters** for professionals all follow established norms.

👉 **Why this matters in business:**
Recognizing and mastering the genres of your field signals that you are a **member of the professional community**. It shows you “speak the language” of your group—whether through speech or writing. This is not just formality; it is credibility and professionalism.

## Business Writing Genres: An Overview

In the **business world**, communication largely happens in writing. Professionals constantly create, read, and interpret documents.

Examples of common **business writing genres** include:

* **Resumes (CVs)**
* **Business letters**
* **Proposals**
* **Reports**
* **Executive summaries**
* **Contracts**
* **Advertisements**

📌 **Note:** Reading, writing, and editing these genres take up a **large portion of a businessperson’s daily work**. This is why genre awareness is not optional—it is a practical skill for survival and success in business.

## How to Differentiate Between Business Writing Genres

Although there are many genres, each can be distinguished by three specific features:

1. **Register**
2. **Format**
3. **Structure**

Let’s go through each in detail.

---

### 1. Register

**Definition:** The *register* of a genre refers to the expected **level of formality** and **type of language** used.

* Think of register as the **tone, vocabulary, and style** appropriate for the context.
* Register varies across business genres:

  * A **business e-mail**: usually semi-formal, clear, concise.
  * A **business contract**: highly formal, legalistic language.
  * A **proposal**: formal but persuasive, using precise vocabulary.
  * A **resume**: factual, concise, using industry-standard phrases.

🔎 **Side Note (Practical Tip):**

* Misusing register can harm credibility. Imagine writing a **contract** with casual language (“Hey, just promising we’ll do this”)—it would look unprofessional.
* Always adapt your register to match audience expectations.

---

### 2. Format

**Definition:** The *format* of a genre refers to the **visual design** or **layout** of the text on the page.

* Formats are often standardized. In fact, many are available as **templates**.
* For instance, a **business letter**:

  * Receiver’s address at the top
  * Date
  * Greeting (“Dear Mr. Santos,”)
  * Body paragraphs
  * Closing salutation (“Sincerely,”)
* If you visualize how it *looks on the page*, you’re thinking about its format.

🔎 **Side Note (Key Difference):**

* *Format = what it looks like* (appearance, layout, margins, placement).
* This is why MS Word and Google Docs often have pre-made templates for letters, resumes, or reports—formats are conventional and widely recognized.

---

### 3. Structure

**Definition:** The *structure* of a genre refers to the **set of topics** or **logical organization** of content expected in the document.

* Structure is about **what information must be included** and how it flows.
* Example:

  * A **business letter** might include: greeting, purpose of letter, background details, request or information, closing.
  * A **CV/Resume** includes: education, work experience, skills, and references.

🔎 **Side Note:**

* Unlike format, which is about *visual design*, **structure is about content expectations.**
* If you include irrelevant topics (e.g., inviting someone to lunch inside your CV), you break the genre structure and confuse the reader.

---

### Register vs. Format vs. Structure

Let’s clarify further to avoid confusion:

* **Register** = Language style (formal, informal, persuasive, factual).
* **Format** = Page design (where elements appear, templates, layout).
* **Structure** = Content organization (topics expected in that genre).

👉 A good way to visualize:

* **Register** = tone of voice.
* **Format** = clothing style.
* **Structure** = body skeleton (underlying framework).

## Why This Matters for Business Professionals

* Mastery of **register, format, and structure** makes your communication **clear, professional, and credible**.
* Misalignment (wrong register, poor format, or broken structure) creates confusion, slows down business, and damages reputation.

📌 **Example:**
Imagine submitting a **proposal** that looks like a casual e-mail, with no clear sections, no headings, and vague language. Even if your idea is good, your document will likely be dismissed.
