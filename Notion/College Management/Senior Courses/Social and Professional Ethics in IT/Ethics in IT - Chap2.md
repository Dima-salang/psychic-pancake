# **CHAPTER 2: ETHICS FOR IT WORKERS AND IT USERS**
## **A Feynman-Style Lecture: "What Do You Care What Other People Code?"**

---

## **I. THE BIG PICTURE: IT WORKERS ARE SPECIAL**

Alright, here's the thing. If you're an IT worker, you have something that most people don't have: **power over the machines that run everything.**

Think about it. You can:
- See data that others can't
- Change systems that control money, health, votes
- Access secrets, private messages, trade secrets
- Break things or fix things

With that power comes responsibility. Not just legal responsibility—**ethical responsibility.** Because laws are slow, and technology moves fast. You need to know what's right even when nobody's watching.

---

## **II. THE SIX RELATIONSHIPS YOU MUST MANAGE**

As an IT worker, you're not working alone. You have relationships with six different groups. Each one has its own ethical pitfalls.

### **1. IT Worker ↔ Employer**

**The Core Tension:** They pay you. You have access to their secrets. They want results. You want to do things right.

**Key Ethical Issues:**

| Issue | What It Means | Real Example |
|-------|-------------|--------------|
| **Software piracy** | Using unlicensed software to save money | Company uses 37% unlicensed software globally —IT staff often know and don't stop it |
| **Trade secrets** | Taking company secrets when you leave | Engineer downloads proprietary code before quitting for competitor |
| **Whistleblowing** | Reporting illegal/unethical company actions | See something dangerous, management ignores it, what do you do? |

**The Piracy Problem (2024):**
- Global software piracy: 37% of installed software unlicensed 
- Cost: $46.3 billion annually 
- IT workers often the enablers—either actively pirating or looking the other way

**Why it matters:** Pirated software often contains malware. One incident costs average $2.4 million to fix, 243 days to resolve . You're not saving money—you're creating risk.

---

### **2. IT Worker ↔ Client**

**The Core Tension:** Client trusts you to solve their problem. You have expertise they don't. Information asymmetry creates ethical risk.

**Key Ethical Issues:**

| Issue | What It Means | Red Flag |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| **Conflict of interest** | Recommending your own products when better alternatives exist | "You need my company's solution, trust me" |
| **Fraud/Misrepresentation** | Lying about capabilities or progress | "The project is on schedule" (it's not) |
| **Breach of contract** | Not delivering what was promised | Scope creep, missed deadlines, hidden problems |

**Why projects fail (and who's at fault):**
- **Scope creep:** Client keeps asking for more, you keep saying yes without adjusting timeline/budget
- **Poor communication:** You speak tech, they speak business—misunderstanding ensues
- **Legacy systems:** Client didn't tell you about old system that must connect to new one
- **Outdated solution:** By the time you finish, technology has moved on

**The ethical rule:** When you know something's going wrong, **say something.** Silence is complicity.

---

### **3. IT Worker ↔ Supplier**

**The Core Tension:** They want to sell you stuff. You decide what to buy. Gifts and favors can blur judgment.

**Key Ethical Issue: Bribery**

| What It Looks Like | Why It's Wrong |
|-------------------|--------------|
| Vendor takes you to expensive dinner, game, trip | Creates obligation to buy from them |
| Vendor gives you "consulting fees" for recommending them | You're being paid to betray your employer's trust |
| Vendor offers job if you steer contract their way | Future gain corrupts present judgment |

**The Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA):**
- US law prohibits bribing foreign officials
- Also requires accurate books and internal controls
- Applies to any US company or company listed on US exchange
- Penalties: Up to $2 million per violation for companies; $100,000 and 5 years prison for individuals

**2024 reality:** AI is now being used to detect bribery patterns in procurement data .

---

### **4. IT Worker ↔ Other Professionals**

**The Core Tension:** You're part of a profession. You have obligations to fellow professionals, but also competition and pressure to succeed.

**Key Ethical Issues:**

| Issue | What It Means | Why It Hurts Everyone |
|-------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Résumé inflation** | Lying about skills, experience, certifications | Underqualified person gets job, fails, damages profession's reputation |
| **Inappropriate information sharing** | Gossiping about proprietary info from previous job | Violates confidentiality, legal liability, professional trust |

**Résumé inflation stats:**
- 30% of US job applicants exaggerate accomplishments
- 10% "seriously misrepresent" backgrounds
- Most common lies: skills (57%), responsibilities (55%), employment dates (42%) 

**The ethical rule:** Your reputation is your most valuable asset. One lie can destroy it.

---

### **5. IT Worker ↔ IT Users**

**The Core Tension:** Users need technology to work. You control that technology. They depend on you.

**Your Responsibilities:**
- Understand user needs (don't just impose solutions)
- Deliver quality products
- Establish ethical environment (piracy policies, appropriate use)
- Protect users from themselves (security, privacy)

---

### **6. IT Worker ↔ Society**

**The Core Tension:** Your code affects everyone. Social media algorithms, medical devices, voting systems, financial networks—you're building infrastructure that shapes society.

**The Ethical Imperative:** Consider the broader impact of your work. Who benefits? Who's harmed? What are the unintended consequences?

## **III. BECOMING A PROFESSIONAL: FOUR PATHS**

### **1. Professional Codes of Ethics**

**What they are:** Written principles guiding professional conduct.

**Why they matter:** They provide:
- Common framework for ethical decisions
- Standards of practice
- Public trust
- Self-assessment benchmark

**Major IT Codes:**
| Organization | Focus |
|-------------|-------|
| **ACM** (Association for Computing Machinery) | Computing professionals broadly |
| **IEEE-CS** | Computer science and engineering |
| **AITP** | IT management professionals |
| **SANS** | Information security |

**ACM Core Principles (2024):** 
- **Honesty:** Be transparent about capabilities, limitations, problems
- **Integrity:** Act with responsible decision-making
- **Confidentiality:** Maintain appropriate secrecy
- **Accountability:** Take responsibility for actions and moral implications
- **Respect for Intellectual Property:** Don't steal others' work
- **Fairness:** No discrimination; foster inclusive participation

---

### **2. Professional Organizations**

**Why join?**
- Network with peers
- Stay current with developments
- Access to resources, training, conferences
- Professional credibility

**2024 trend:** AI ethics is now central to professional development . Organizations like ISACA offer courses on "Ethics in AI" for auditors, risk managers, and governance professionals.

---

### **3. Certification**

**What it is:** Third-party validation of specific skills/knowledge.

**2024 landscape:** 
- **Skills-based hiring rising:** 19% of US job posts don't require degree (up from 15% in 2021)
- **Micro-credentials:** Short, focused certifications for specific skills (96% of employers value them) 
- **Performance-based exams:** Moving from multiple-choice to real task demonstration 
- **Continuous validation:** No more "one-and-done"; ongoing learning required 

**Hot certifications (2024):**
- AI/ML: Azure AI Engineer, Google Professional ML Engineer
- Cybersecurity: CCSP, OSCP
- DevSecOps: Kubernetes Administrator (CKA), DevOps Engineer Expert 

**The catch:** Certification ≠ competence. It's a starting point, not proof you can do the job.

---

### **4. Licensing**

**What it is:** Government permission to practice profession (like doctors, lawyers, engineers).

**The case for licensing IT professionals:**
- Modern systems are complex, interconnected, safety-critical
- Software controls cars, planes, medical devices, power plants
- Failure can kill people
- Licensing would enforce standards, create accountability

**Current status (2024):**
- **Software engineering licensing exists:** 40+ US states offer it through NCEES 
- **Requirements:** Accredited degree, 4 years experience, pass two exams
- **Reality:** Most IT workers practice under "industrial exemption"—work internally without license
- **Trend:** Growing but not universal

**The malpractice question:** If you're licensed and mess up badly, you can be sued for professional malpractice—just like a doctor or engineer.

## **IV. ETHICAL ISSUES FOR IT USERS**

### **Common Problems:**

| Issue | What It Is | Why It Matters |
|-------|-----------|--------------|
| **Software piracy** | Copying software without license | Illegal, creates security risks, hurts innovation |
| **Inappropriate use of resources** | Using work computers for personal stuff | Wastes time, creates liability, security risk |
| **Inappropriate information sharing** | Leaking private/confidential data | Violates privacy, legal liability, damages trust |

**The remote work complication (2024):**
- 45% of remote employees feel less accountable 
- 60% admit to "bending rules" when remote 
- 50% cite lack of supervision as reason 

**But also:** Ethical leadership works remotely too. Studies show ethical leaders boost remote employee performance and commitment .

## **V. ENCOURAGING ETHICAL BEHAVIOR: WHAT ORGANIZATIONS CAN DO**

### **1. Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)**

**What it is:** Document stating what users can and can't do with IT resources.

**Five key elements:**
1. **Purpose:** Why the policy exists
2. **Scope:** Who and what is covered
3. **Policy:** What's allowed and prohibited
4. **Compliance:** How monitored, who's responsible
5. **Sanctions:** Consequences for violations

### **2. Technical Controls**

| Control | What It Does |
|---------|-------------|
| **Firewalls** | Block unauthorized access |
| **Access controls** | Limit who can see what |
| **Encryption** | Protect data in transit and at rest |
| **Monitoring** | Detect suspicious activity |
| **Data loss prevention** | Stop unauthorized data exfiltration |

### **3. Compliance and Audit**

**Key players:**
- **Audit committee:** Board-level oversight of financial reporting and compliance
- **Internal audit:** Assess controls, verify compliance, recommend improvements
- **Information security (infosec) group:** Manage security processes and tools

**2024 AI twist:** AI is now used to analyze whistleblower reports, detect patterns, and automate compliance monitoring .

## **VII. THE BOTTOM LINE: FEYNMAN'S SUMMARY**

1. **IT workers have power.** Power over data, systems, infrastructure. With power comes ethical responsibility.

2. **Relationships matter.** You have obligations to employers, clients, suppliers, colleagues, users, and society. Each relationship has its own ethical pitfalls.

3. **Professionalism is earned.** Through codes of ethics, organizations, certifications, and potentially licensing. It's not just about technical skills.

4. **Users need guidance.** Clear policies, technical controls, and ethical culture help people do the right thing—especially in remote work environments.

5. **Ethics is practical.** It's not abstract philosophy. It's about concrete decisions: Do I report this bug? Do I accept this gift? Do I blow the whistle? Do I copy this software?

6. **The easy way is often the wrong way.** Shortcuts, lies, looking the other way—they catch up with you. Integrity is doing the right thing when nobody's watching.

---

**Remember:** The question isn't "Can I get away with this?" The question is "Is this the right thing to do?" And right thing is defined by your professional obligations, not just your personal convenience.
