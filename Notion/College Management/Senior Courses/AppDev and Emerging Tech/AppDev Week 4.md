## 💡 Lecture 8: Introduction to Systems Analysis and Design

Welcome, class. Today we launch into the discipline that ties together everything we've discussed: **Systems Analysis and Design (SA&D)**. This field is the bedrock of Information Technology (IT) and Software Engineering, focused on strategically developing high-quality Information Systems that drive business value.

---

### 1. Defining IT and SA&D

We begin by recalling the foundational definition of IT:

* **Information Technology (IT):** You may recall from your introductory courses that IT is **a combination of Hardware, software, products and services**. It is fundamentally used **to manage, access and communicate and share information**.
* **The IT Environment:** The nature of IT is **dramatic and continuous** due to the constant advances that **influence change in business organization**. This dynamism is why a structured approach is essential.
* **Systems Analysis and Design (SA&D):** SA&D is **a step-by-step process for developing high quality Information Systems**.

### 2. Information Systems Components

As we discussed previously, an Information System (IS) is more than just a computer program. **Information Systems includes technology, people and data performing certain business functions.**

#### A. Data and Information
* **Data:** **Data is raw facts** (or **raw data**).
* **Information:** **Information are processed data**—data organized, structured, and presented in context to make it useful.

#### B. The Five Components Revisited
The lecture illustrates that **an information system needs these components**:

* **Hardware:** The physical equipment; it **has limitations** that developers must account for (e.g., processing speed, memory).
* **Software:** **Those who manages the hardware components** and includes **application software from operations with horizontal, vertical and Legacy systems in terms of the development**.
    * **Horizontal System:** Software usable across many different industries (e.g., accounting software).
    * **Vertical System:** Software unique to a specific industry (e.g., hospital patient management system).
* **Data, Processes, and People:** **Information systems are made up of data, processes and people.**
    * **People (Stakeholders):** **Those individuals that can benefit or their interest in is affected** by the system.
* **Example:** A **typical payroll system** demonstrates this, where **data is stored in Separate Tables that are linked to form an overall database.** 

### 3. The Role of the Systems Analyst

The **systems analyst** is the key role in bridging the gap between business needs and technological solutions.

* **Core Responsibilities:** Systems analysts **plan, develop and maintain information systems**. They also **manage IT projects with meetings, presentations, memos, reports and documentations**—emphasizing the communicative and managerial nature of the role alongside the technical aspects of it.
* **System Definition:** A **system is defined as a set of related components that produces specific results.**
* **Mission Critical:** Systems that are **vital to operations** are known as **Mission critical systems**.

### 4. Strategic Business Context

Systems development occurs within a dynamic business environment influenced by several macro-level trends:

* **Globalization:** **Rapidly increasing globalization** demands systems capable of handling multiple languages, currencies, and regulatory environments.
* **Integration:** **Technology integration for seamless information access** means systems must communicate easily with each other (interoperability).
* **Cloud Computing:** The **rapid growth of cloud-based Computing and services** has fundamentally changed system deployment and scalability.

#### A. The Internet Model
The internet provides the standard architecture for modern systems: **we have e-commerce, we have UI that communicates between database management software and a web-based server.** 

* **E-Commerce:** E-commerce involves combinations of **B2C** (Business-to-Consumer) and **B2B** (Business-to-Business). Its foundation was built upon **electronic data interchange (EDI)**, but today, modern systems rely heavily on **Supply Chain management** for **managing of inventory cost and the supplies**.

#### B. Business Profile and Process
To build a system, the analyst must first understand the client's world:

* **Business Profile:** **Overview of the company** including its **mission, functions, organizations, product Services, customers, suppliers, competitors, constraints and future Direction.**
* **Business Process:** A **business process model** uses standard representations like **Business Process Modeling Notation (BPMN)** to illustrate **a simple business model that might consist of an event, three processes and a result.** 

### 5. Types of Business Information Systems

Modern organizations use an interconnected portfolio of systems to manage operations:

* **Office Productivity Systems:** **All employees use Office productivity systems** (e.g., **Microsoft Office**).
* **Enterprise Computing:** This **supports company-wide operations and data management requirements**, most notably through **ERP or the enterprise resource planning** systems (like SAP or Oracle).
* **Transaction Processing Systems (TPS):** The **DPS** (**Data Processing Systems**) or **TPS** handles daily transactional data. The lecture illustrates that **a single sales transaction consists of six separate tasks which the TP or TPS processes assembled.**
* **Business Support Systems:** These assist management (e.g., **MIS** - Management Information Systems) and operations (e.g., **radio frequency identification (RFID)** for tracking assets).
* **Knowledge Management (KM):** Systems focused on capturing, storing, and sharing organizational expertise, often utilizing **knowledge basing** and **inference rules** to **identify data patterns and relationships**.
* **User Productivity Systems:** Systems designed to **improve productivity with the use of technology and groupware** (for **data sharing**).
* **Systems Integration:** The ultimate goal, where different system types combine: **a combination of transaction processing, business support, Knowledge Management and user productivity features.**

## 🎯 Lecture 9: Analyzing the Business Case and Assessing Feasibility

Welcome back, class. In Systems Analysis and Design (SA&D), before we begin any technical work, we must first analyze the **Business Case**. This critical step justifies the project, ensuring that our efforts align with the organization's strategic goals and are feasible from multiple perspectives.

---

### 1. Strategic Planning and Alignment

Every systems project must support the long-term vision of the organization.

* **Strategic Planning:** This is the process of **identifying long-term organizational goals, strategies and resources** to guide decision-making.
* **Guiding Documents:** To ensure alignment, we use the business's **mission statement**, which **reflects the vision, purpose and values** as well as the **critical success factor** (CSF) or high priority objectives.
* **The IT Role:** The **role of the IT department is to provide careful project planning**. The work should **support the overall business strategy and operational needs of the company**. The project **scope must be well defined and clearly stated and goals should be realistic and type specific statements**.

#### SWOT Analysis (Strategic Planning Tool)
The **SWOT analysis is a framework to understand the current situation of the business**. It stands for the four areas to be inspected: **strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats**.

* **Internal Factors:** **Strengths and weaknesses refer to the internal condition of the business** (e.g., specific patents, staff expertise, legacy technology).
* **External Factors:** **Opportunities and threats are external factors that can improve or impair it** (e.g., new markets, competitor moves, regulatory changes). 

[Image of a SWOT analysis framework showing internal (Strengths, Weaknesses) and external (Opportunities, Threats) factors]


---

### 2. The Business Case and System Requests

The **business case** is the **justification for a proposal**. It must be **comprehensive and easy to understand**.

* **Purpose:** It should **describe the project clearly, provide justification to proceed and estimate the financial impact of the project**.
* **Core Questions:** It must answer essential questions like: **Why are we doing this project? How much will it cost and how long will it take? Are there any risks involved?** and **How will we measure success?**

#### System Request
A **systems request is a starting point for most information system projects**. It is a **formal way of asking for IT support**.

* **Six Main Reasons for Requests:** System requests typically seek improvements such as **stronger controls, reduced cost, more information, better performance, improve service or more support** for new products and services. 

#### Processing the System Request
Requests are typically handled by a committee to ensure fair evaluation:

* **Committee:** There should be a committee that **handles Computer Resources which evaluates that request**.
* **Forms:** Requests are submitted using **forms to be submitted with clear instructions, supporting documents and should be easy to understand**.
* **Challenge:** The **disadvantage of having a committee include having delays** and the possibility that **members favor projects** due to individual bias.

---

### 3. Assessing Request Feasibility (The Four Factors)

After receiving a request, the committee assesses its **feasibility** to determine if the project can, and should, proceed. The initial assessment is based on answering four questions: **Would it be easy to learn and use? Will benefits exceed costs? Do we have the Technical Resources? and Can we do it in time?**

This is broken down into four detailed types of feasibility:

#### A. Operational Feasibility
Checks **whether the proposed system will be used effectively after it has been developed**. It is concerned with how the system fits into the organizational culture and procedures.

* **Key Questions:** **Is the project supported by Management in users?** Will a new system **result in Workforce reduction?** Do **legal or ethical issues need to be considered?**

#### B. Economic Feasibility
Ensures that **projected benefits of a post-system outweigh total cost of ownership (TCO)**.

* **Total Cost of Ownership (TCO):** Determining TCO requires analysis of all costs, including **the people involved, hardware and Equipment, software, formal and informal training, licenses and fees, Consulting expenses and facility costs**.
* **Costs and Benefits:**
    * **Tangible:** Costs **measured in dollars or other currencies**; benefits result from a **decrease in expenses or an increase in revenues**.
    * **Intangible:** Costs **can significantly affect organizational performance** (e.g., loss of customer goodwill); benefits are **important to the company despite the inability to measure them in dollars or currencies** (e.g., improved decision making).

#### C. Technical Feasibility
Refers to the **Technical Resources required to develop and operate the system**.

* **Key Questions:** **Does the company have the necessary Hardware, software and network resources?** **Does the company have the required technical expertise?** Will the **superposed platform have sufficient capacity for future needs?**

#### D. Schedule Feasibility
Determines if the **project should be implemented in an acceptable time frame**. The key issue here is the **interaction between time and costs**.

* **Key Questions:** **Can the company or the IT team control the factors that affect schedule feasibility?** **Has management established a firm timetable?** **What conditions must be satisfied?**

---

### 4. Factors Affecting System Projects

Projects are influenced by both internal and external forces:

* **Internal Factors:** These are forces within the company: **strategic plan, top managers, user requests, IT Department, existing systems and data, company finances.**
* **External Factors:** These are external market and regulatory forces: **government, technology, suppliers, customers, competitors and the economy.**

## 📈 Lecture 10: Setting Priorities and Conducting the Preliminary Investigation

Welcome back, class. In our continuing discussion on analyzing the business case, we move past the initial request and into the systematic process of **setting project priorities** and conducting the **Preliminary Investigation**. This phase transforms a simple request into a defined, justified project plan.

---

### 1. Setting Priorities: Discretionary vs. Non-Discretionary

In any organization, system requests constantly compete for limited resources. A formal process for prioritizing is essential, especially since priorities are **dynamic**—they can change based on business factors.

#### A. Factors that Affect Priority
The decision to prioritize a project is based on its potential impact:

* **Cost & Revenue:** Will the proposed system **reduce costs** or **increase Revenue**? (Economic Impact).
* **Information & Results:** Will the system project **result in more information or produce better results**? (Operational Impact).
* **Service & Organization:** Will the system **serve customers better**? Will the system **serve the organization better**? (Strategic Alignment).
* **Feasibility:** Can the project **be implemented in a reasonable time period**? Are the **necessary Financial, human and Technical Resources available**? (Resource Feasibility).

#### B. Types of Projects
System projects fall into two categories based on managerial choice:
* **Discretionary Projects:** These are projects **where management has a choice in implementing them**. These are typically new feature enhancements or strategic initiatives.
* **Non-Discretionary Projects:** These are projects **when management has no choice but to implement them**. **Many of these projects are predictable**, such as **annual updates to payroll tax percentage** or **quarterly changes** due to regulatory compliance. These must be prioritized and scheduled.

---

### 2. The Preliminary Investigation

The Preliminary Investigation is the first step in the SDLC (Systems Development Life Cycle) after a request is received. Its goal is to analyze the system request and determine the project's feasibility and scope before committing significant resources.

#### A. Planning the Investigation
When planning the investigation, the analyst holds a meeting with **key managers, users and I.T staff**. The analyst should **describe the project, explain responsibilities, answer questions and invite comments**. Crucially, during this planning, the analyst must **focus on improvements and enhancements not problems**.

#### B. The Six Steps of the Preliminary Investigation
The investigation follows a structured six-step process:

1.  **Understand the Problem or Opportunity**
    * Develop a **business profile that describes current business processes and functions**.
    * **Understand how these modifications will affect business operations and other information systems**.
    * Identify the **Department's, users and business processes involved**.
    * To effectively present the data, the analyst should **consider using a fishbone diagram** (Ishikawa diagram) to visualize potential causes of a problem. 

[Image of a Fishbone Diagram illustrating potential causes of a system problem]


2.  **Define the Project Scope and Constraints**
    * **Define the specific boundaries or the extent of the project**.
    * Create a list detailing what the system **must do, should do, could do and won't do**.
    * **Avoid project creeps** (the tendency for the project scope to expand uncontrollably) and **identify constraints**.
    * Constraints are categorized by **timing** (e.g., implemented **now** or in the **future**), **type** (e.g., **external or internal constraints**), and **urgency** (e.g., **mandatory or desirable**). 

3.  **Perform Fact Finding**
    * Gather data about project **usability, costs, benefits and schedules**.
    * Techniques used include **analyze organizational charts, to conduct interviews, to review the documentation, to observe operations, conduct a user survey and analyze the data**.

4.  **Analyze Project Feasibility, Cost, Benefit, and Schedule Data**
    * This step formalizes the data gathered in Step 3. The analyst determines **what information must be obtained and how will it be gathered and analyzed**.
    * This is the resource estimation stage: **how much will it cost to analyze the information and prepare a report with findings and recommendations?**

5.  **Evaluate Feasibility**
    * This is the formal assessment of the four key feasibility factors: **the operational, technical, economic and scheduled feasibility**.

6.  **Present Recommendations to Management**
    * The analyst must **summarize the preliminary investigation in the form of a report to show management**.

#### C. Preliminary Investigation Report Structure
The final report is critical for management decision-making. It must include:

* **Introduction** to the investigation.
* **System requests summary**.
* **Findings** of the investigation.
* **Recommendations** (Proceed, Redefine, or Drop).
* **Project rules of the people involved**.
* **Time and cost estimates**.
* **Expected benefits**.
* **Supporting documents in the appendix**.

---

### 3. Conclusion

The Preliminary Investigation successfully determines the viability of a system request by balancing strategic priority with technical and financial feasibility. It is the gatekeeper to the main, resource-intensive analysis and design phases of the SDLC.

## 📊 Lecture 11: Managing System Projects—Planning and Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)

Welcome back, class. We now pivot from analyzing system needs to the discipline of **Project Management**, which ensures that the system is developed efficiently and successfully. Project management is the framework that converts the requirements and feasibility study into a completed, working system.

---

### 1. Overview of Project Management

**Project management** refers to **planning, scheduling, monitoring and controlling and Reporting on information system development.**

#### A. Criteria for a Successful Project
A successful project must meet four essential criteria:
1.  It must be **completed on time**.
2.  It must be **completed within budget**.
3.  It **meets requirements**.
4.  It **satisfies users**.

#### B. The Project Triangle (Triple Constraint)
These three primary constraints define any project: **scope, cost and time of a project.** 


* **Interdependence:** Any **change in one of these legs will affect the other legs**. For example, increasing the **Scope** will necessitate more **Time** and/or more **Cost**.
* **The Challenge:** The project manager's job is to find the **optimal balance** between these three factors while maintaining quality.

#### C. Responsibilities of a Project Manager
The Project Manager's role is multi-faceted, covering the entire project lifecycle:
* **Project Planning:** **Identify all project tasks and estimate completion time and costs.**
* **Project Scheduling:** **Creating a specific timetable showing tasks, task dependencies and critical tasks that might delay the project.**
* **Monitoring:** **Guiding, supervising and coordinating the project team's workload.**
* **Project Reporting:** **Creates regular progress reports for management, users and the project team itself.**

---

### 2. Creating a Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)

A **Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)** is created by **breaking down a project into a series of smaller tasks.** This hierarchical decomposition is fundamental for planning and scheduling.

#### A. Tasks and Milestones
* **Task/Activity:** **Any work that has a beginning and an end and requires the use of company resources such as people, time or money is called a task or activity.** Tasks **should be small and manageable** and are the lowest level in a WBS.
* **Event/Milestone:** These are **recognizable reference points used to monitor progress.** They represent the completion of a major phase or a set of tasks (e.g., "Requirements Analysis Completed").

#### B. Tools for Scheduling
Once the WBS is defined, specific tools are used to schedule and monitor the tasks:

* **Gantt Chart:** This is a **horizontal bar chart representing a set of tasks which shows planned and actual progress on the project.** It is excellent for visually simplifying and communicating the overall project timeline. 
* **Program Evaluation Review Technique (PERT):** This technique **utilizes a bottom up technique and is useful for scheduling, monitoring and controlling actual work** that displays **complex task patterns and relationships** using a network diagram.
* **Critical Path Method (CPM):** Developed by private industry, CPM identifies the **Critical Path**—the sequence of dependent tasks that determines the earliest completion date of the project.

#### C. Estimating Task Duration
The estimation process is typically iterative and collaborative:

1.  The Project Manager **must list them and embed them in a document.**
2.  They **must estimate the task duration.**
3.  Estimates should include the uncertainty: **the best case, probable case and worst case estimates.**
4.  The manager then **assigns a weight to each estimate and calculates the task duration** using a weighted average formula (like the PERT formula).

---

### 3. Factors Affecting Task Estimation

Accurate estimation requires considering external and internal variables that influence productivity:

* **Project Size:** This involves identifying **all project tasks and time required.** Analysts must **consider time taking for events affecting productivity** (e.g., meetings, waiting for approvals).
* **Human Resources:** Project managers **must assemble and guide a development team that has the skill and experience to handle the project.** They must also **deal with factors that could affect the schedule** (e.g., team availability, conflict).
* **Experience:** Managers should **use experience with similar projects where the development time and cost estimates are based on the resources used for similar previously developed Information Systems.** This uses historical data to improve future accuracy.
* **Constraints:** The WBS and schedule must realistically achieve the **definition of system requirements that can be achieved realistically within the required constraints** (scope, time, and cost).

---

## 🗓️ Lecture 12: Managing System Projects—Task Patterns, Critical Path, and Control

Welcome back, class. In this lecture, we finalize our discussion on Project Management by focusing on the logic of scheduling, identifying the **Critical Path**, and implementing techniques for **monitoring and controlling** the project to ensure on-time delivery.

---

### 1. Task Patterns and Dependencies

Effective scheduling requires understanding the **task patterns**, which refers to the **arrangement of tasks in a logical sequence due to the existence of dependent tasks**.

* **Task Box:** To define a task clearly, you can use **task boxes** in a model. Each box contains essential information, including the **task Name, ID, duration, start date, and finish date**.

#### A. Types of Dependencies
Dependencies dictate the order in which tasks must be performed:

| Dependency Type | Description | Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dependent Tasks** | **Tasks that can only be initiated after the prior task has been completed** (Finish-to-Start). | Sequential flow. |
| **Multiple Successor Tasks** | **Tasks that can be initiated simultaneously after a task has been done.** This is termed **concurrent** work. | Allows for parallel work streams. |
| **Multiple Predecessor Tasks** | **Requires the two or more prior tasks to be completed before the initiation of another one.** | Creates a synchronization point in the schedule. |

* **Accuracy:** The **schedule will not be accurate if the underlying task pattern is incorrect**, especially on a large project where **the facts must be studied very carefully to understand the logic and sequence**.

---

### 2. The Critical Path

The **critical path** is the backbone of the project schedule. It refers to a **series of tasks in which if delayed will affect the completion date of the overall project.**

* **Impact:** **If any tasks on the critical path falls behind schedule the entire project will be delayed.**
* **Calculation:** To calculate the critical path, you must first **review patterns and determine start and finish dates which will Define the critical path** using techniques like PERT/CPM.
* **Focus:** **For tasks in the critical path project managers spend most of their time tracking the tasks along the critical path.**



---

### 3. Project Monitoring and Control

Project managers must **monitor and control work** where they **anticipate problems, avoid them and minimize the impact**.

#### A. Techniques for Monitoring and Review
* **Structured Walkthrough:** This is **a review of a project team members work are reviewed by other team members.** These reviews are conducted **throughout the SDLC** and are also known as **peer reviews** or code/testing reviews.
* **Meetings:** Managers must **schedule regular meetings** to **share updates, discuss common problems, explain new techniques and help collect data**. In the Agile methodology, they can use the **daily scrum meetings**.
* **Project Status Reports:** Project managers **must regularly communicate with supervisors, upper management or users and clients** using these reports.
* **Problem Handling:** When problems arise, the manager must already have **potential solutions and select the best way to solve the problem to maintain the schedule**.

#### B. Project Management Tools
Fortunately, **software and tools in project management** aid in planning, scheduling, monitoring, and reporting.

* **Features:** Most of these programs offer **PERT, CPM and Gantt chart, resource scheduling, project calendars and cost tracking**. Software like Microsoft Project provides all these views.

---

### 4. Risk Management

A disciplined approach to risk is essential for managing project uncertainty.

* **Risk Management Plan:** Project management requires a plan to **identify and mitigate risks**.
* **Key Steps in Risk Management:**
    1.  **Developing a risk management plan.**
    2.  **Identifying the risks** (what could go wrong).
    3.  **Analyzing them in qualitative or quantitative way** (how likely and severe are they).
    4.  **Creating a risk response plan, which is a proactive effort to anticipate a risk and describe an action plan to deal with it.**
    5.  **Monitor those risks** throughout the project life cycle.

---

### 5. Managing for Success

**Project management is a challenging task**; managers must be **alert, technically competent and highly resourceful**.

* **Reasons for Delay:** **Projects get delayed for a wide variety of reasons**—some common ones include **business, budget or scheduling issues**.
* **Necessity:** **Project management is needed to be able to remove or avoid these issues** by providing structure, control, and early warning systems.

This concludes our module on Systems Analysis and Project Management. We have covered the strategic decision to undertake a project, its feasibility analysis, and the detailed planning required for execution.
